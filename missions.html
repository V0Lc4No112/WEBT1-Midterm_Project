The six landings

Each crew stayed on the Moon longer and brought back more rock than the crew before it.

Crews and landing sites

  1. Apollo 11

    July 1969, Sea of Tranquility

    Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin made the first landing. Michael Collins stayed in orbit.

  2. Apollo 12

    November 1969, Ocean of Storms

    Pete Conrad and Alan Bean landed close to the old Surveyor 3 probe. Richard Gordon stayed in orbit.

  3. Apollo 14

    February 1971, Fra Mauro

    Alan Shepard and Edgar Mitchell explored the hills of Fra Mauro. Stuart Roosa stayed in orbit.

  4. Apollo 15

    July 1971, Hadley–Apennine

    David Scott and James Irwin were the first to drive the lunar rover. Alfred Worden stayed in orbit.

  5. Apollo 16

    April 1972, Descartes Highlands

    John Young and Charles Duke landed in the highlands. Ken Mattingly stayed in orbit.

  6. Apollo 17

    December 1972, Taurus–Littrow

    Eugene Cernan and Harrison Schmitt made the last landing. Ronald Evans stayed in orbit.

Where is Apollo 13? It launched in April 1970, but an oxygen tank exploded on the way to the Moon. The crew flew around the Moon and came home safely without landing.
